Understanding Prostate Health: What Every Man Over 50 Should Know

A clear, practical guide to the most common prostate problems, the warning signs to watch for, when to get screened, and the everyday habits that support a healthy prostate.

Why This Matters

The prostate tends to grow as men age. For many men, this leads to bothersome urinary symptoms that slowly affect sleep, comfort, and quality of life — and are often ignored for years.

About half of men in their 50s have an enlarged prostate — and that number rises to as many as 9 in 10 men over 80.

What Is the Prostate?

The prostate is a small gland, about the size of a walnut, located just below the bladder. It surrounds the urethra — the tube that carries urine out of the body. That's why, when the prostate grows, it can squeeze the urethra and make urination harder.

Enlarged Prostate (BPH)

A non-cancerous growth of the prostate. Very common with age and the main cause of urinary symptoms in older men.

Prostatitis

Inflammation or infection of the prostate. It can cause pain, burning, and discomfort, and can occur at any age.

Prostate Cancer

One of the most common cancers in American men. Often grows slowly and may cause no symptoms in early stages.

Common Warning Signs

Talk to your doctor if you notice any of these:

  • Waking up more than once a night to urinate
  • A sudden, urgent need to go
  • Weak or interrupted urine stream
  • Trouble starting to urinate
  • Feeling that the bladder isn't fully empty
  • Dribbling at the end of urination
  • Pain or burning when urinating

Are You at Higher Risk?

Your risk of prostate problems is higher if you:

  • Are 50 years of age or older
  • Have a father or brother who had prostate problems or prostate cancer
  • Are African American (higher risk of prostate cancer)
  • Are overweight or physically inactive
  • Have type 2 diabetes or heart disease
  • Eat a diet high in red meat and processed foods

Screening: When to Talk to Your Doctor

Two common checks are the PSA blood test and a physical exam of the prostate. Whether and when to screen is a personal decision to make with your doctor:

AgeGeneral Guidance
40 – 54Ask your doctor early if you're African American or have a family history
55 – 69Discuss the benefits and risks of PSA screening with your doctor
70+Routine screening is generally not recommended — decide with your doctor

A high PSA level doesn't always mean cancer — it can also rise with an enlarged prostate or inflammation. Results must always be interpreted by a healthcare professional.

Daily Habits That Help

🥦

Eat More Plants

Fill your plate with vegetables, fruits, beans, and whole grains. Tomatoes, broccoli, and leafy greens are great choices.

🐟

Choose Healthy Fats

Favor fish, olive oil, and nuts over red meat, fried foods, and processed meats.

🚶

Stay Active

Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week — like a brisk 30-minute walk, 5 days a week.

🌙

Limit Evening Fluids

Drink plenty during the day, but cut back 2 hours before bed to reduce nighttime bathroom trips.

☕

Watch Caffeine & Alcohol

Both can irritate the bladder and make urgency and frequency worse.

🩺

Get Checked Regularly

Don't ignore urinary changes. Early conversations with your doctor lead to more options.

When to See a Doctor Right Away

  • You can't urinate at all
  • Blood in your urine or semen
  • Fever or chills with painful urination
  • Persistent pain in the lower back, hips, or pelvis

If you take medication for your prostate, never stop or change it without talking to your doctor first.
